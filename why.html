The Basics

What exactly are microgreens?

Microgreens are the seedling stage of vegetables, herbs, and greens — harvested just 7 to 21 days after germination, right after the first true leaves emerge. This is the precise moment when a plant's nutritional content is at its absolute peak.

Unlike sprouts (which are germinated seeds eaten whole, root and all), microgreens are grown in a growing medium, harvested above the root line, and eaten as a green. They sit between a sprout and a baby green — and they're in a nutritional league of their own.

A landmark study published in the Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry found that microgreens contain up to 40 times more vital nutrients than their mature counterparts. This isn't a marketing claim — it's peer-reviewed science.

Sweet · Tender · Lush

Full, lush pea shoot tendrils — among the most beloved by chefs for their clean sweetness and satisfying texture. Exceptional source of vitamins A, C, and folate. Beautiful draped over fish, pasta, or spring vegetable dishes.

Nutty · Crunchy · Rich

One of the most beloved by chefs. Thick, juicy stems with a rich nutty flavor and satisfying crunch. Exceptional source of plant protein, healthy fats, and vitamin E. Beautiful on composed salads, grain bowls, and summer plates.

The Nutrition Science

What's actually
inside these tiny leaves.

The reason microgreens are so nutrient-dense is biological. In the first days of a plant's life, it mobilizes all the energy and nutrients stored in its seed to fuel rapid growth. This is when concentration is highest — before nutrients get distributed across a full-grown plant.

What this means for you:

Vitamin C — Immune support, collagen synthesis, powerful antioxidant. Found in very high concentrations in red cabbage, broccoli, and radish microgreens.

Vitamin K — Critical for bone health and blood clotting. Microgreens like amaranth and chard contain extraordinary amounts.

Vitamin E — A fat-soluble antioxidant that protects cells from oxidative damage. Sunflower microgreens are particularly rich sources.

Sulforaphane — Found in broccoli microgreens, this compound is one of the most studied natural anti-cancer agents known to science.

Polyphenols & Antioxidants — Fight inflammation, support cardiovascular health, and slow cellular aging. Present across all microgreen varieties.

Beta-Carotene — Converts to vitamin A in the body. Supports vision, skin health, and immunity. Abundant in sunflower and pea shoot microgreens.
