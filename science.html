The Foundation

Why microgreens are different
from everything else in your fridge.

In 2012, a team of USDA Agricultural Research Service scientists published a landmark study analyzing 25 commercially available microgreen varieties. Their findings were clear: microgreens contain dramatically higher concentrations of vitamins and carotenoids than their mature counterparts — on average about five times more, and in some cases up to 40 times more for specific nutrients.

The reason is biological. In the first days of a plant's life, all of the energy and nutrition stored in the seed is mobilized to fuel rapid growth. This is the moment of peak concentration — before nutrients spread across roots, stems, and a full canopy of mature leaves. Harvest at this moment and you capture the most nutrient-dense version of that plant that will ever exist.

A 2025 study published in Scientific Reports (Nature) confirmed these findings across six varieties including beet, sunflower, radish, pea, and broccoli microgreens — with sunflower showing the highest calcium levels, broccoli the highest iron and phenolic compounds, and pea shoots excelling in phosphorus and copper.

Sources

The research
we relied on.

Every number on this page traces back to peer-reviewed science or official government databases. We encourage you to read the originals.

  • 01
    USDA Agricultural Research Service — "Specialty Greens Pack a Nutritional Punch" (2014)
    Summary of the landmark 2012 study analyzing key nutrients in 25 microgreen varieties. The primary source for our variety-specific Vitamin C, E, and K data.
  • 02
    Scientific Reports (Nature) — "Nutritional quality profiles of six microgreens" (2025)
    Peer-reviewed analysis of broccoli, radish, beet, pea, sunflower, and bean microgreens. Confirmed and expanded on the 2012 USDA findings with updated mineral data.
  • 03
    Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry — "Microgreens: Assessment of Nutrient Concentrations" (2012)
    The original peer-reviewed study. Full data tables for all 25 varieties including ascorbic acid, beta-carotene, phylloquinone, and tocopherol concentrations.
  • 04
    CDC — Defining Powerhouse Fruits and Vegetables (2014)
    Centers for Disease Control ranking of nutrient-dense foods. Watercress ranked #1 with a perfect nutrient density score of 100.
  • 05
    Microgreen Directory — Nutrition Facts Database
    Aggregated microgreens nutrition reference database drawing from multiple peer-reviewed sources.
